### Tables

- `matches`: one row per (gameid, teamid). Holds year, date, split, league, teamname, side, gamelength (seconds), and result. `year` is the calendar year of `date` (since 2026-10-07): Oracle's Elixir files promotion and qualifier games under the season they qualify for (Aug–Sep 2026 PRM, LFL and CBLOL games carried 2027), which made a team's season label jump back and forth. Rows Oracle's Elixir leaves without a `teamid` (about 500 team rows in 2026, mostly smaller teams in LIT, LPLOL, CD and LJL) get the id the same team name has elsewhere in that year's file, else `name:<teamname>` (`002_add_matches.fill_team_ids`, since 2026-10-07; before, 1,850 games kept only one team and dropped out of Elo and FORGE).
- `match_stats`: team totals per game (gold, kills, towers, objectives, firsts, vision).
- `match_players`: each team's five starters per game (gameid, teamid, position, playerid, playername); `playerid` falls back to `name:<playername>|<teamid>` (0.7% of rows). Covers every game, unlike `player_stats`.
- `player_stats`: per-player snapshots at 10/15/20/25 minutes, including the lane opponent (`opp_*` columns are always the same-role player on the other team), and the champion. Games without snapshots (Oracle's Elixir "partial" data) are dropped; the 20- and 25-minute columns are NULL when the game ended first (before 2026-10-02 those games were dropped, which removed about three quarters of games under 25 minutes). `playerid` uses the same fallback as `match_players`, but AURA joins `match_players` on (gameid, teamid, position) for its ids.
- `match_glory_stats` (view): per-minute and per-10-minute rates used as GLORY features.
- `game_length_elo`: per-game pre/post Elo per team, keyed on (gameid, teamid), with the team's `home_league`, that league's `league_offset` after the game, and `main_elo` (the main roster's rating, used by forecasts). Without the key every pre-game Elo join made SQLite build a temporary index.
- `game_length_player_elo`: every rostered starter's rating before and after each game (offset included), keyed on (gameid, playerid). Each team row in `game_length_elo` is the mean of its five rows here.
- `game_length_elo_league_offsets`: each league's offset after every international game that moved it (gameid, date, league, league_offset).

## Season stats and forecasts

The metrics come in two families, judged differently:

- **Season stats** (GLORY; sunset: Record, Luck, GLORB, unadjusted GLORY) describe a team-season with hindsight. They may use every game of the season, weight games equally, and show small samples as they are. They are judged by `evaluate_season_stats.py`: split-half reliability (random halves by game id, Spearman-Brown, games to reach 0.5) within-season correlation with win % and Record, and correlation of the stat on one half with win % on the other half (out of sample, so a stat can't score well just by restating results).
- **Forecasts** (FORGE, Elo; Form, sunset as a page, is FORGE's input) predict the next game from earlier games only, weight recent games more and shrink small samples. They are judged only by `evaluate_metrics.py`. No forecast takes a season stat's output as input.

**Change a metric only for a significant benchmark gain.** A change to how a published metric is computed (features, weights scheme, hyperparameters, method) ships only when its benchmark beats the current version on the same games or team-seasons by more than noise: for forecasts, the paired-bootstrap 95% interval of the domestic log-loss difference (`evaluate_metrics.py`) must exclude 0 and international log loss must not get significantly worse; for season stats, a paired bootstrap over team-seasons of split-half reliability or r with the other half's win % (`evaluate_season_stats.py`) must exclude 0, with neither getting significantly worse. A gain inside the noise is not a reason to change. Record each tested change and its numbers in the work log either way. Refitting the existing weights on new data (`--write-weights`) is not a metric change.

**Accuracy fixes follow a lighter rule.** A change that corrects which data a forecast uses, without changing how the metric is computed (a team's home league, which roster stands for a team, a mislabelled league or event), is an accuracy fix, not a metric change. It ships when it is significantly better on the games it affects, by a paired bootstrap on the benchmark that covers them (`evaluate_metrics.py`'s cross-league section, or our calls in `evaluate_markets.py`), and neither domestic nor international log loss gets significantly worse.

**AURA follows the same rule on player benchmarks** (`evaluate_aura.py`). A change to how AURA is computed (snapshot, features, weights, credit scheme, matchup adjustment) is compared with the current AURA on the same player-seasons and team-seasons, with weights fit on earlier years only and any tuning constant fixed in advance. It ships only when all of these hold: (1) a gain: the paired-bootstrap 95% interval excludes 0 for next-season r of players who changed team, or for split-half reliability, but a split-half gain counts only if the correlation of a player's score with their teammates' in the same game rises by no more than 0.05 (a score that moves with the team becomes stable by restating it); (2) it beats its team-only control (the added part replaced, for every player, by their team's average of it in that game) on the gain it claims, by a paired bootstrap, so the gain is the player's and not the team's; (3) neither split-half nor new-team r gets significantly worse, and the roster test (r with the other half's win %, paired over team-seasons) doesn't either; (4) calibration stays close (ECE under 0.01) for a model that uses only in-game snapshots. A model with end-of-game stats as features is not judged on calibration, because those stats build up for the winner. The substitution test is reported but doesn't decide on its own: teams choose their substitutes, so it is confounded and noisy. Once AURA feeds a forecast (Player Elo, FORGE), that change falls under the forecast rule above, with the backtest's roster-change slice reported.

## GLORY algorithm (as implemented)

For each year:
1. Fit `StandardScaler → LinearRegression(result)` on all major-league games that year.
2. Average each team-season's features, keeping only team-seasons with at least `minimum_matches` games.
3. Score = `pipeline.predict(averages) * 100`.
4. GLORB instead sums the scaled features, z-normalizes them, and maps them to mean 80 and SD 15.
5. Add `era_score` (z within the year) and `league_score` (z within league and year).

`before=<date>` restricts both the fit and the averages to earlier games (used by the backtest).

Each year's major-league games are read once per call and used for both the fit and the averages (a separate read only happens when the league filter includes non-major leagues). `load_glory_games()` reads every year up front so several calls can share it through `games=`; `build_site.py` does this for its rankings.

### Opponent adjustment

The published GLORY is `opponent_adjusted="record"`: for each year and feature, fit `feature ~ own_rating + opp_rating` over that year's major-league games, where ratings are each team's full-season Record (log-odds), then restate each game against the average opponent (`f - b_opp * (opp_rating - mean)`) before averaging. The pipeline is still fit on the unadjusted games. Pass `record=` (a `season.get_record` frame) to share one Record fit. `opponent_adjusted="elo"` (or `True`) is the retired GLORY+, which used each opponent's pre-game Elo; the season-stat report still scores it for comparison. The unadjusted GLORY is published on the sunset page `glory_unadjusted.html`; `glory_plus.html` redirects to GLORY.

## Record and Luck (sunset)

Both leave the header and team pages but keep their pages. Record is still computed every build because GLORY's opponent adjustment uses it.

`prometheus/season.py`.

- **Record** (`get_record`): per year (`matches.year`), a Bradley-Terry model over every game in every league, including international events. Each game is two weighted rows: a win with weight s and a loss with weight 1 − s, where s is Elo's game-length winner score from the team's side (`elo.winner_score`). Ridge prior: ratings ~ N(0, `RECORD_PRIOR_SD`² = 1) in log-odds (`LogisticRegression(C=1)`, no intercept). Ratings are centred on the mean major-league team (home league = most-played domestic league that year) and published as `100 · sigmoid(rating)`. The site shows the same team-seasons as GLORY, with GLORY's league label.
- **Luck** (`get_luck`): per year, GLORY's per-game pipeline (unadjusted) predicts each major-league game's result, clipped to [0, 1]. A team-season's raw earned win % is the mean prediction; it is calibrated with a games-weighted linear fit of season win % on raw earned % across that year's team-seasons (the per-game linear model pulls every team toward 50%). Luck = win % − earned, as points and as wins (`luck_wins`).

## Form

`prometheus/form.py`. Per team-game GLORY stats for every league (`load_form_games`), then:

1. `opponent_adjust`: each stat is restated against an average opponent by pre-game Elo, `f − b_opp × (opp_elo − mean)`, with `b_opp` from `f ~ own_elo + opp_elo` fit on earlier seasons only (the first season uses its own games).
2. `form_states`: in date order, each team keeps an exponentially weighted sum of stats (`HALF_LIFE` = 20 games); at a new `matches.year` its sum and weight are multiplied by `CARRY` (0.5). The pre-game state mixes in the season's running average (`PRIOR_GAMES` = 5 games' worth). A missing stat counts as average. Home league is the domestic league the team has played most that season (a cup or international event doesn't change it). Returns pre- and post-game states per row.
3. `scores`: state · Form weights (logistic, per unit of stat gap, fit on domestic blue-minus-red pre-game gaps; `form_weights.json`).
4. `league_relative`: score minus the mean pre-game score of the same home league and season through that day.

Form on the site is league-relative Form in Elo points (`FORM_POINTS` = `form_weight / elo_weight` per log-odds unit).

## FORGE

FORGE (Form + Elo) was called GlorELO+ until 2026-10-02; the module, weights file, CLI metric (`forge`) and column names were renamed with it.

`prometheus/forge.py`. Same home league: P(win) = sigmoid(`ELO_WEIGHT` × Elo gap + `FORM_WEIGHT` × Form gap), so the rating is Elo + `FORM_POINTS` × Form and P = sigmoid(`ELO_WEIGHT` × rating gap). Each league rates on its own scale: `blend_weights(league)` gives the pair (a, b) (the majors' `ELO_WEIGHT`, `FORM_WEIGHT`; every other league the pooled `OTHER_LEAGUE_FORGE_ELO_WEIGHT`, `OTHER_LEAGUE_FORM_WEIGHT`, so per-league pairs can drop in there), the rating is Elo + (b / a) × Form and `forge_ratings` returns a as each row's `slope`. Different home leagues: P = sigmoid(`CROSS_REGION_ELO_WEIGHT` × Elo gap), because Form only compares a team with its own league (blending it into cross-region games made them worse in the backtest). `team_forms` gives now and season-end Form per team; `forge_ratings` joins Form with Elo on team name (and year). Weights are fit by the backtest and stored in `forge_weights.json`; `forecast.js` gets `CROSS_REGION_ELO_WEIGHT` and each league's slope (`weights.leagues`, from `site.registers.forge_register`) through the page config. `evaluate_metrics.py --write-weights` refreshes both weight files; `--check-weights` prints every change and warns (GitHub annotation) when a blend weight moves more than `WEIGHT_TOLERANCE` (10%); Form's per-stat weights are printed but never warn, because some are near zero.

## Elo

`prometheus/elo.py`, replayed in date order by `004_bootstrap_elo.py`. Ratings belong to players. A team's rating is the average of its five starters' ratings (`match_players`) plus its home league's offset. The home league is the non-international league the team has played most this calendar year, with last year's home counting as one game (a tie keeps the current home), so a cup, promotion series or EMEA Masters doesn't relabel it. Each game changes every starter's rating by the team's K=20 × (actual − expected), where the winner's "actual" falls from 1.0 for very short games to 0.65 for very long ones. When teams from two leagues meet at an international event, each league's offset also moves by `LEAGUE_SHARE` (0.5) of the team's change. A new player starts at the average rating of the league's active players (last game in that league within `ACTIVE_DAYS`, 365), or 1500 plus the offset if there are none; a player who changes league keeps their rating. A team with no roster for a game uses its last roster, and one that never had one plays as a single "player" (plain team Elo; this is what unit tests without rosters exercise). Each team row also stores `main_elo`: the team's rating with its main roster (the starters of its last game in its home league or at an international event) after the game, so an academy lineup fielded under the team's name at a cup doesn't stand in for the team. `get_latest_elos` and `get_season_elos` use `main_elo` (falling back to `post_match_elo`) and the stored `home_league`, and add each league's offset changes since the team's last game. `compute_elo_records(..., player_records=True)` also returns each starter's ratings, which `bootstrap_elo` stores; `get_player_history` reads them with names, roles and teams, and `get_player_elos` gives each player's current and calendar-year-end rating (offset moves included, as for teams). `get_season_elos` gives every team's rating at the end of each calendar year it played (last game that year plus offset changes through year end), labelled with its most-played domestic league that year. Calendar years, not Oracle's Elixir `year`, because some autumn games are filed under the next season.

## Forecast pages

`build_site.py` gives the Elo, Form (sunset) and FORGE pages two kinds of rows, flagged `now`. Now rows are current ratings, `active` when the team played within `ACTIVE_WINDOW` (183 days) of the newest game (Elo and Form: every team; FORGE: active teams in every league, international events excluded as home leagues). Season rows are team-seasons at year end (Elo: `get_season_elos`; Form: the state after the team's last game that year against that league-season's average; FORGE: that Form plus season-end Elo). Team pages reuse the FORGE season rows for their FORGE column and the now rows for the fact line; GLORY comes from rankings with `minimum_matches=1`. Record and Luck (sunset) are no longer on team pages. `rankings.js` shows active now rows until seasons are picked, and searches all now rows. The head-to-head box uses now rows only. The FORGE page config sets `leagueScales`, so `rankings.js` shows one scale at a time (the majors by default, or one league; a name search reaches every league and ranks each team within its own scale). The home page reuses the FORGE now rows of the major leagues (head-to-head box and top 10) and the Team and Player Elo now rows (top 10 active), with bars on each page's scale (`_rating_bar`).

Players (`player_pages_and_rows`): listed players have played in a major league or at an international event, or anywhere within `PLAYER_PAGE_WINDOW` (730 days) of the newest game; `dup:` ids are never listed. `player_elo.html` (config `entity: "player"`) has a now row per listed player and season rows for major-league player-seasons with `PLAYER_SEASON_GAMES` (10) or more games. Each listed player gets `players/<slug>.html`: slug from the name, then name plus last team for shared names, then a number. Career rows are stints (consecutive games for one team id). `players.json` feeds the header search with `teams.json`. `team_rosters` gives each team page its last lineup (the five starters of its latest game, by role, with today's player Elo) and its starters by calendar year and role (main starter first, then up to `ROSTER_EXTRAS` = 3 others with games started), grouped by team name like the team pages. `names.js` holds the JS shared by `search.js` and `rankings.js` (`fold`, `slugify`, `rank`); `base.html.j2` loads it before `search.js`. Team and player pages embed their Elo series as `[date, elo]` pairs (`compact_series`).

## Game logs

`prometheus/gamelog.py`, called by `site.gamelogs.add_game_logs` after the team and player pages are built. `load_team_games` reads every team-game with its opponent and Elo; `add_calls` adds the pre-game chance and method (pre-game Elo from `game_length_elo`, pre-game Form from the build's `form_states` made league-relative with `form.league_relative`, then `schedule.game_probability`'s rule: cross-region Elo between home leagues, FORGE within a league (`forge.other_league_forge_probability` outside the majors), Elo on the league's curve (`forge.other_league_weight`) when either team has no Form state); `add_series` groups a team's consecutive games against one opponent within `SERIES_GAP` (12 hours). `series_heads` gives each series its date, league, opponent, score, series chance (game 1's chance through `schedule.series_probability` with the best-of read off the score) and Kalshi's price (`PriceBook`: same two teams, start within `MARKET_WINDOW` = 36 hours, nearest; prices from `data/market_prices.json` via `load_prices`, plus prices saved in the prediction log). `load_player_games` merges starters, champions and player Elo from the build's `PlayerTables` (see *Player tables*). `team_logs` and `player_logs` build compact JSON (short keys, empty fields and the default method left out; documented in their docstrings); `year_records` gives each year's series and game record. `site.gamelogs.game_log_context` embeds the newest `GAME_LOG_SERIES` (20) series in the page (`#games-data`) and writes the whole log to `output/games/{teams,players}/<slug>.json` when there are more; `site_static/js/entry.js` renders both, loads the file on "Show every series" or a season jump, and marks the current section in the "On this page" list (`templates/_entry.html.j2`, styles in `site_static/css/entry.css`). `scripts/export_market_prices.py` writes `data/market_prices.json` from `evaluate_markets.py`'s saved `frames.pkl`.

## Player tables

`prometheus/player_tables.py`. SQLite has no index for the joins between `player_stats`, `match_players`, `matches` and `<method>_player_elo`, so joining them in SQL took about 27 seconds across the build's three player reads. `PlayerTables(method, stats, minutes)` reads each table's columns once, on first use (`roster`, `stats` with the champion and the `<stat>_<minute>` lane gaps computed in SQL, `elo`, `matches`), and `aura.load_aura_games`, `elo.get_player_history` and `gamelog.load_player_games` merge from it in pandas (about 8 seconds of reads). `build_site.py` makes one and passes it to all three; called alone, each makes its own. `get_player_history` merges from the roster side so ties within a game keep the order the SQL join gave; all three return exactly what the SQL versions did.

## AURA

`prometheus/aura.py`. `load_aura_games` takes every starter's lane gaps (own minus lane opponent) for gold, XP, CS, kills, deaths and assists at each snapshot, keeping games where both teams have all five starters. `game_frame` turns them into one row per game from the blue side (30 columns, role × stat); `fit_aura_weights` fits a logistic regression (C=1 on standardized gaps) of blue's win on them. Blue's log-odds is the intercept plus five lane terms, so a player's snapshot score (`player_scores`, `snapshot_scores`) is their lane's term, and the two players in a lane get opposite scores. `get_aura` adds the late part: each player's term change from 15 to 25 minutes (20 when the game ended first; 0 when it ended before 20), team-centred (`team_centred`: own change minus the four teammates' mean, so it sums to 0 over a team), times `LATE_WEIGHT` (0.25), giving `aura` and `aura_late`. Weights are fit per year; `earlier_only=True` fits each year on every earlier year (years with fewer than `MIN_TRAIN_GAMES` earlier games are left NaN), which the held-out check uses. `season_aura(scored, leagues)` gives one row per (player, year, role) in the major leagues: last name, the team and league played most, games, AURA in win-chance points (`POINTS` = 25 × mean log-odds), and for seasons with `SEASON_GAMES` (20) or more, Role Z, role rank and the role's count. `site.players.aura_rows_and_seasons` keeps listed players: qualified seasons become `aura.html` rows (kind `season`, entity `player`, a symmetric domain, a role filter via `filters.roles`), and every season goes on the player's page (`aura_seasons`).

`scripts/evaluate_aura.py` reports calibration (weights fit on every earlier year, scored on each year from 2022, per snapshot) and, for major-league player-seasons, split-half reliability, correlation with teammates in the same game, and year-to-year correlation for players who stayed on a team and players who moved, against the 15-minute lane gold gap and team win %, with paired bootstraps (`evaluation.paired_correlation_bootstrap`). Correlations are centred within year and role. Its held-out section (`held_out`) compares AURA with the 15-minute term alone, both with `earlier_only` weights, on major-league seasons from 2017, as the AURA change rule asks. Two team-level tests: the **roster test** (`roster_table`) sums each starter's AURA from one half of a team-season (shrunk as if 5 average games were added; 0 for players who didn't play that half) over the lineups the team used in the other half, and correlates it with that half's win %, against GLORY's halves (imported from `evaluate_season_stats.glory_stats`), the team's own AURA and win %, with a paired bootstrap over team-seasons. The **substitution test** (`substitutions`) takes games where exactly one starter changed from the team's previous game, and correlates incoming minus outgoing AURA so far (shrunk mean of each player's last 50 games before it, every league; `so_far`) with the result minus Player Elo's expected result. `pregame_gap_r` is its reference: the same AURA-so-far sums as a team gap over all games.

## Backtest

`scripts/evaluate_metrics.py` (helpers in `prometheus/evaluation.py`) scores forecasts only: blue side, win % so far (baseline), Elo as of each month's start, live (pre-game) Elo, live Form and live FORGE. A month is scored when both teams have 5+ major-league games that season before it. Each metric's win curve is fit on the other seasons; Form's stat weights are too (`add_form`). FORGE uses a curve on Elo and Form gaps for same-league games (fit on same-league games) and the Elo curve for the rest (`forge_probabilities`). Scores: accuracy, Brier, log loss, paired bootstrap against win % so far, domestic and cross-region international games reported separately. "Elo (as of cutoff)" is `evaluation.elo_as_of`: each team's rating after its last game before the cutoff plus its home league's offset moves since then (from `game_length_elo_league_offsets`), matching `get_latest_elos`. "Elo (live)" is each game's pre-match rating. "Team Elo, no player ratings" replays the same Elo without rosters (`compute_elo_records` without `rosters`) as a reference, and a last table compares the two on domestic games early in a season and within 10 games of a starter change (`load_roster_context`). A final section, "Cross-league, every league" (`load_cross_league`, `evaluation.cross_league_games`), scores Elo (live) on every game at any event between teams whose home leagues differ, the home being the non-international league a team played most that season (whole-season homes only choose the games; they never feed a forecast), split into major v major, major v other and other v other, and international events vs EMEA Masters, cups and promotion. It is the benchmark for league offsets below the majors. "Within other leagues" (`evaluation.other_league_games`: both teams' season home is the non-major league the game was played in) scores Elo with the standard 400-point curve, one curve fit on the other seasons, and a curve per league fit the same way (`evaluation.out_of_year_league_probabilities`), paired. Per-league slopes come from `evaluation.shrunk_league_slopes`: each league's logistic slope through the origin, shrunk toward the pooled slope by empirical Bayes (DerSimonian-Laird between-league variance against the league's own sampling variance), so nothing is tuned and a small league ends near the pooled slope. Its last table, "FORGE within other leagues" (`summarize_other_league_forge`), adds Elo + Form on one curve fit on the other seasons, with the Form gap from `out_of_year_form` (Form's stat weights fit on major-league games of the other seasons, as for the domestic backtest), against both Elo curves. `published_weights` fits `other_league_elo_weight` (pooled), `other_league_forge_elo_weight` and `other_league_form_weight` (Elo and Form) and the per-league slopes (`league_curves.json`) on all of those games; `--check-weights` prints the per-league changes but warns only on the blend weights.

Shared statistics in `prometheus/evaluation.py` (since 2026-10-08): `resamples(units, n_resamples, seed)` yields bootstrap row positions, over rows or whole clusters (team-seasons, groups of games), one draw per resample from one seeded generator; `interval(values)` is the 95% percentile interval; `ece(p, won, bins)` is the expected calibration error. `paired_bootstrap`, `paired_correlation_bootstrap`, `compare_benchmarks._rows_bootstrap`, `evaluate_aura.roster_bootstrap` and the market benchmark's ROI and blend intervals use them (each report checked byte-identical before and after).

## Benchmark comparison (PR check)

`evaluate_metrics.py`, `evaluate_season_stats.py` and `evaluate_aura.py` take `--dump DIR`, which writes the per-unit data behind their headline comparisons without changing the report: `forecasts.csv.gz` (per section, forecast and game: gameid, teamid, year, won, log loss, Brier, accuracy, for Domestic, International, Cross-league and Within other leagues), `halves.csv.gz` (per stat and team-season: full, h0, h1, n0, n1) and AURA's held-out `halves`, `moved`, `roster` and per-snapshot `calibration` CSVs, each with a `metrics.json` of headline numbers. `evaluate_markets.py --dump DIR` writes `calls.csv.gz` (per scored series and map 1: kind, Kalshi event ticker, team1, method, major, our call `p`, result `won1`); with `--offline` it fetches nothing (markets not in the cache go unpriced), so two runs on one cache score the same markets. `evaluate_markets.py --update-cache` refetches both market lists and the candlesticks of every settled series and map 1 not yet cached, with no DB (publish.yml's `kalshi-cache` job). `scripts/compare_benchmarks.py BASE HEAD` reads `metrics/`, `season/`, `aura/` and `markets/` under each directory, pairs base and head on shared keys (repeated keys numbered in order), counts rows only one side has, and reports head minus base with a 95% paired bootstrap: log loss per forecast and section (`evaluation.paired_bootstrap`), season-stat split-half r (`paired_correlation_bootstrap`, centred within year on the paired sample) and r with the other half's win % (each side's own win % halves; team-seasons resampled), AURA split-half and new-team r (`paired_correlation_bootstrap`) and roster r (team-seasons resampled with both halves), each snapshot's ECE, and our market calls' log loss per kind and slice (series: all, FORGE major, FORGE other league, other; map 1: all; slices by the head's method; paired over markets). It exits 1 when a guarded value is significantly worse: domestic, international, cross-league and within-other-league log loss of the published forecasts (FORGE and Elo (live); Elo per league and FORGE within other leagues), GLORY's two numbers, AURA's three correlations, an AURA ECE above 0.01, or our calls' log loss on every series, FORGE major-league series or map 1 (`GUARDED_FORECASTS`, `GUARDED_STATS`, `ECE_LIMIT`, `GUARDED_MARKETS`). A benchmark missing from one side (no Kalshi cache yet) is noted, not compared. It checks only the "not significantly worse" half of the rules; whether a change earns a gain is still judged from the reports.

## Predictions

`prometheus/schedule.py`, called by `site.predictions.update_predictions` after the Form pass.

- **Schedule.** `fetch_schedule` reads Leaguepedia's `MatchSchedule` joined with `Tournaments` (match id, UTC start, teams, best-of, winner and scores, event name and league) in pages of 500. Anonymous clients are rate-limited after a few quick calls; `_get` backs off (20, 40, 60, 90, 120 s) and then gives up, and logs in first when `LEAGUEPEDIA_USER`/`LEAGUEPEDIA_PASSWORD` (a bot password) are set, in the environment (CI secrets) or, for local builds, in a gitignored `.env` at the repo root (`schedule.credentials`; the environment wins). A build fetches 3 days back and 7 ahead, or 30 days back when the log is new. Any failure leaves the log as it was and the site builds with yesterday's calls; `PREDICTIONS_FETCH=0` skips the fetch.
- **Names.** `TeamMatcher` maps a Leaguepedia name to a `get_latest_elos` team name: an alias (`prometheus/team_aliases.json`, package data), the exact name, the folded name (accents and punctuation dropped; letters with no accent to strip spelled out, so LØS folds to "los", the same in `names.js`), then the folded name without Leaguepedia's "(… Team)" disambiguation; the most recently active team wins a tie. On a 966-match sample (2 Sep–10 Oct 2026) 452 matches had both teams rated; nearly every miss is a team Oracle's Elixir doesn't cover (Komplettligaen, CELOL, LCP Wildcard, Game Changers, Benelux, national teams).
- **League label.** `event_league`: two teams of one league at that league's own event show under it (Leaguepedia files the LPL regional finals under the World Championship); else the event's league mapped to an Oracle's Elixir code (`EVENT_LEAGUES`); else the teams' shared home league, "Intl" for two leagues, or whichever is known. `is_major` (the home page's set): an international event, or a major league's event with a team from that league (not an LCS promotion series between challengers).
- **Odds.** `team_ratings` joins today's Elo (`get_latest_elos`) with Form; `game_probability` uses FORGE within a major league, `CROSS_REGION_ELO_WEIGHT` × Elo gap between leagues (as the head to head), and within any other league `forge.other_league_forge_probability` (`OTHER_LEAGUE_FORGE_ELO_WEIGHT` × Elo gap + `OTHER_LEAGUE_FORM_WEIGHT` × Form gap; Form is stored in Elo points, so it is divided by `FORM_POINTS`), or, when a team has no Form (`has_form`), `forge.other_league_weight(league)` × Elo gap: that league's slope from `league_curves.json`, or the pooled `OTHER_LEAGUE_ELO_WEIGHT`. Method `forge` covers both FORGE cases; alerts (`alerts.select`) and the market tables' FORGE group (`schedule._group`, else "Other") keep to teams whose `home1` is a major league. `series_probability` assumes independent games.
- **Log.** `data/predictions.json` (`PREDICTIONS_LOG`), one entry per Leaguepedia match id. `update_log`: before the start a match's call is replaced with today's (stamped `predicted` and `data_through`); after it the saved call is frozen; a match first seen after its start (or saved unmatched) gets a call from `RatingsBefore(states)(day)` (Form from earlier games, Elo as of day − 1), marked `reconstructed`. `RatingsBefore` gives exactly `ratings_before(states, day)`, cached by day, but reads the Elo history once (`elo.LatestElos`, the same rows as `get_latest_elos(method, date)` for any date from one read) instead of querying it for every day (about 1 s each). Results (winner, scores) are copied whenever Leaguepedia has them. A 30-day backfill takes about a minute (about 1.7 s a day, the dated Elo query). CI keeps the log in the backup bucket (deployment.md).
- **Site.** `predictions_view` splits matched entries into upcoming and past, builds `fixture_row`s (series and one-game chances as whole numbers adding to 100, the favourite, our names and team-page slugs, result and call) grouped by UTC day, the home page's next `HOME_FIXTURES` (10) major and international matches within `HOME_FIXTURE_DAYS` (4), `schedule.scorecard` (series and games picked, log loss per game, for saved, reconstructed and all calls), `schedule.market_scorecard` (settled matches priced before the start and 12 hours out: ours against Kalshi's last price per series, paired-bootstrap difference from `MARKET_MIN_SERIES` = 30) `schedule.edge_record` (both split by `BET_GROUPS`, FORGE / Elo × saved calls / backtest of rebuilt calls, and grouped for the page by `site.predictions.method_groups`, with the backtest's dates from `backtest_span`; paper bets 12 hours out by edge from `market_12h`, the last read at least `markets.BET_LEAD` before the start, kept by `attach_prices` and `update_log` and backfilled by the script: return after fees and CLV with intervals) and `alert_record` (the alerts' CLV and ROI after fees, from `alerts.settled_alerts`). `predictions.json` in `output/` publishes every matched entry. The page lists results from the last `RECENT_RESULT_DAYS` (3); `result_months` and `render_results` write `results/YYYY-MM.html` for every month with a played match. `scripts/oneoff/extend_log.py` (one-off) adds rebuilt calls for matches before the log's first (`--since`, schedule fetched a month at a time and cached) through `update_log`, as a new log's 30-day rebuild does. `predictions.js` sets local times and regroups the days, and filters by league and team.
- **Market prices.** `prometheus/markets.py` (shared with `scripts/evaluate_markets.py`): after `update_log`, `schedule.attach_market_prices` reads Kalshi's open `KXLOLGAME` markets (one paged call to the public API, no key), resolves Kalshi names with `TeamMatcher` plus `markets.MARKET_ALIASES`, and `markets.attach_prices` gives every logged match that hasn't started the market between the same two teams starting nearest within 18 hours: `market` = {`p` (team 1's chance: the mean of team 1's bid-ask mid and one minus team 2's, over quotes with a spread of 0.10 or less), `spread`, `at`, `ticker`}. `update_log` carries an upcoming match's last `market` over when it rebuilds the entry, and a started match keeps its entry whole, so the last price before the start stays. Any Kalshi error, or `KALSHI_PRICES=0`, leaves prices as they were. Kalshi's API refuses browsers from other origins (403), so prices are only as fresh as the build. `fixture_row` adds `mkt1`/`mkt2`/`mkt_at` and `mkt_url` (`markets.event_url`: `https://kalshi.com/markets/kxlolgame/league-of-legends-game/` plus the event ticker in lower case); the register draws the caret and the Kalshi column, whose pair links to that page.
- **Hourly prices.** `scripts/update_prices.py` (run by `prices.yml`) reads the log, fetches Kalshi's open markets once, and runs `markets.attach_prices` with a `TeamMatcher` over the teams in the log (every team we could price is there), so it needs no database. It then runs `alerts.update`, saves the log, and writes `kalshi.json` (`markets.prices_file`: when prices were read and, per rated match from two days back, team 1's and team 2's chance, when read, the market's URL) and `predictions.json` (`markets.published_log`) for upload. The daily build writes the same `kalshi.json` into `output/`, so a deploy doesn't delete it. `predictions.js` fetches `kalshi.json` (no-store) and updates each fixture row by its `data-match` id: the caret, the Kalshi pair and link, the tooltips, and a "Kalshi prices as of …" note in the upcoming register's caption, in local time. Team page links in the issue come from the live `teams.json`. A run takes about 2 seconds of Python.
- **Alerts.** `site.predictions.write_kalshi_alert`, after the predictions update: only when this build's Kalshi fetch priced matches (`coverage["at"]` is when) and unless `KALSHI_ALERTS=0`. `alerts.select` takes FORGE calls (`method == "forge"`) within a major league (`home1` a major) starting 6–36 hours later whose price was read in this build, skipping a call whose ratings lack a team's settled result from more than `schedule.STALE_HOURS` (36) before now (`schedule.missing_results`: the market has seen it, so an edge is likely the data's age), and backs a side when FORGE's series chance minus that side's ask (`market.ask1`/`ask2`, from `markets.buy_costs`) exceeds `alerts.EDGE` (0.05). `alerts.record` stores `alert` {side, p, price, edge, at} on the entry once (a later run keeps the first, as a paper bet would), the log is saved, and `alerts.update` writes `data/kalshi_alert.json` (title, body, `day` key, `comment`, `new`). The issue lists the matches qualifying now plus those alerted earlier that UTC day that haven't started (`alerts.shown`), at current prices, marking an edge that has fallen to 5 points or less as gone; `comment` lists only the matches new since the last run. The issue body has a table per match (start in UTC and Pacific, event, team backed, FORGE, ask, edge, links to Kalshi, the Predictions page filtered to the team and both team pages), the paper record (`alerts.paper_record`: settled alerts as $1 bets after the 7% taker fee, `markets.fee`), how alerts are chosen, and a mention of the owner. Both workflows post it with `scripts/post_kalshi_alert.sh` (deployment.md). A stale alert file is always removed first, so a day without alerts posts nothing. `alerts.update` also runs `select(..., minor=True)` (FORGE within one non-major league) and records those as `paper_alert` on the entry, never in the issue; `paper_record` and `settled_alerts` score them with `key="paper_alert"`.
- **CLI.** `prometheus predict [--days N] [--league X] [--major]` prints the same odds for upcoming matches without touching the log.
- **Price backfill.** `scripts/oneoff/backfill_market_prices.py` (one-off, not in CI): `markets.backfill_prices` matches started, unpriced log entries to settled Kalshi series events as `attach_prices` does and stores the last two-sided hourly quote before the log's start (spread at most 0.10; both contracts averaged) with `backfilled: True` and `at` the candle's end. It reuses `evaluate_markets.py`'s market lists and candlestick cache (`data/markets/`), fetching what's missing one request at a time.
- **Elo margin experiments.** `scripts/research/elo_variants.py` replays player-built Elo in memory (`replay(inputs, margin_fn, K=...)`, winner's score per game from both teams' `match_stats`) and scores it with `evaluate_metrics.py`'s own functions (`score`, `compare`, `held_out`: tune on 2014–2021, report on 2022 on). `load_inputs` takes about 15–30 s, one variant about 4 s. The family scripts (`elo_variants_gold.py`, `_kills.py`, `_objectives.py`, `_combined.py`) and their reports in `docs/` tested 28 margins (2026-10-06): none beat game length.
- **Market benchmark.** `scripts/evaluate_markets.py` (not part of the build) reads Kalshi's public market data (no key): every settled `KXLOLGAME` (series winner, one yes/no contract per team) and `KXLOLMAP` market, live and historical endpoints, parsed from each market's rules text (event, teams, scheduled start in Eastern time; some give only the day, taken as midnight Eastern). Kalshi names go through `TeamMatcher` with `MARKET_ALIASES` (mostly pre-rebrand names). Our call is rebuilt as the log does (`RatingsBefore`, `game_probability`); the best-of and a check of Kalshi's result come from our games between the two teams within a day of the start. The market's chance is the bid-ask mid from hourly candlesticks (one contract per match fetched, the more traded; both when cached), at the last hour before the start and 12 hours before, dropped when the spread is over 0.10. It scores both on series results (and map 1 against our one-game chance) with `evaluation.paired_bootstrap`, fits the result on both log-odds (`combined_weights`), reports calibration, a lead-time ladder (the market at the close and 6, 12, 18 and 24 hours before against our call, with the joint fit at each, on matches with exact starts and a quote at every lead) and a betting section ($1 bets 12 hours before the start at the ask, after Kalshi's fee at the rates its `/series` data lists for `KXLOLGAME` (`markets.fetch_fee_rates`: `fee_type` `quadratic`, so taker 7% × `fee_multiplier` and no maker fee; the table adds a maker row only for a series with maker fees; falls back to taker 7%, no maker fee): back our pick, or value bets above edges of 0, 3, 5 and 10 points, with bootstrap ROI intervals, and closing line value: the closing chance for the team backed minus the price paid, from `bets`/`bet_clv`). A "Saved calls" section (`saved_calls`, `saved_section`) scores the prediction log's calls as made (`--log`, default `data/predictions.json`; not `reconstructed`, settled, priced before the start): `p_series` against `market.p` at the close and `market_12h.ours` against `market_12h.p` 12 hours out, by FORGE major / FORGE other league / Other, with intervals from 10 series; it is recomputed from the log on every run. `--reuse` rewrites the rest of the report from the tables saved in `data/markets/frames.pkl`. Set `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS=1` on macOS: with many BLAS threads one run stalled for 40 minutes. Candlesticks are cached per market in `data/markets/candles/`; Kalshi refuses bursts, so they are fetched one at a time (a first run takes about an hour; later runs about 2.5 minutes; 10 before `RatingsBefore`, 2026-10-07).

## Constraints

- Static hosting only. Anything dynamic must be precomputed at build time or done in browser JS.
- `scripts/download_data.py` downloads only the current year's CSV from Google Drive when `data/raw` has the rest (every year's with `--full`), and keeps the old file unless the new one parses and has newer games. The upstream folder ID is hardcoded there.
- `002_add_matches.py` reads only the columns it uses (`COLUMNS`) and writes with `utils.insert_rows` (one DB-API `executemany`, NaN as NULL), as does `bootstrap_elo`: the DB build went from about 63 to 28 seconds locally on 2026-10-07 with identical table contents (checked table by table; only row order changed, as files are now read in sorted order).
- A site build takes about 45 seconds (plus about 20 for the game logs: every team-game's call, a million player-games, and about 7,000 log files) plus the schedule fetch (a second or two, or minutes when Leaguepedia rate-limits and the client backs off), and about a minute more for a new log's backfill: SQLite reads (each year's games, every game for Record and Form, Elo, about a million player-games, about 900,000 AURA player-games), the Form pass over about 200,000 team-games in Python, AURA (about 15 seconds: four snapshot fits a year), and writing about 2,000 team pages and 4,900 player pages. The forecast backtest takes about 8 seconds and the season-stat report about 5.
- `tests/test_alerts.py`: alert selection (FORGE only, the 6–36 hour window, the edge bar, fresh prices, backing either side), recording an alert once, minor-league paper alerts (recorded, never posted), the paper record after fees, CLV per settled alert, the issue's table and links, `buy_costs`, and `write_kalshi_alert` (writes the issue and log; removes a stale file when prices aren't fresh).
- `tests/test_download_data.py`: which Drive files `download_data.py` fetches (current year, last year in January, all with `--full` or when one is missing) and when a download replaces the old file (only with newer games; never a refused or broken one). `tests/test_schedule.py` covers `missing_results`, and `tests/test_alerts.py` skipping a call whose ratings lack a team's settled result.
- `tests/test_update_prices.py`: one hourly run on a temporary log with a fake Kalshi book (prices, the alert, `kalshi.json` and `predictions.json`), and a run with no markets.
- `tests/test_markets.py`: backfilling started matches, matching open Kalshi markets to the log (team order swapped, thin quotes, a start too far off, map markets, started matches, an unmatched team) and parsing open markets.
- `tests/test_evaluate_markets.py`: Kalshi rules parsing (Eastern time, maps, day-only starts, a colon inside the event name), grouping contracts into matches, quotes from candlesticks (live and historical field names), the market chance from both contracts with the spread cut, series results from our games, value bets with their profit and CLV, and `combined_weights` on synthetic data; no network.
