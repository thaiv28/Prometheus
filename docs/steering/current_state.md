# Current state

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03 (Predictions page). This is a code baseline. Read `db/prometheus.db` or the live site for current rankings.

## Working

- **Data:** Oracle's Elixir CSVs for 2014 to the present, rebuilt from scratch by `scripts/setup_db.sh` and refreshed daily in CI. International events (Worlds, MSI, EWC, First Stand, ...) are ingested as their own leagues (`INTERNATIONAL_LEAGUES`); they are the only games that connect regions.
- **Two families.** Season stats describe a team-season with hindsight and are judged by `scripts/evaluate_season_stats.py` (stability and fit to that season's results; `docs/season_stats_report.md`). Forecasts predict the next game from earlier games only and are judged by `scripts/evaluate_metrics.py` (`docs/metric_backtest.md`). No forecast uses a season stat as input.
- **Season stats** (LCK/LPL/LEC/LCS team-seasons):
  - GLORY: per-year regression weights on gold and objective rates, with every game's stats adjusted for the opponent's full-season Record; about 0–100, with Era Z and League Z. Full-season reliability 0.91; about 8 games to reach 0.5.
  - Record (sunset 2026-10-01; page kept, still GLORY's opponent-strength input): a schedule-adjusted results rating (Bradley-Terry on game-length-weighted results, every league including international events), shown as the chance to beat the average major-league team that year. Reliability 0.89.
  - Luck (sunset 2026-10-01; page kept): wins above what a team's play earned (GLORY's per-game model, calibrated per season). Reliability 0.34, so it is mostly luck; it correlates 0.23 with win %.
  - Sunset (`sunset.html`, linked from the header's Teams menu): Record, Luck, GLORB, GLORY unadjusted (`glory_unadjusted.html`), Form (folded into FORGE), and GLORY+ (folded into GLORY; `glory_plus.html` redirects).
- **Forecasts:**
  - Game-length Elo for every team in every region, built from player ratings: a team's rating is the average of its five starters plus its league offset; new players start at the average of their league's active players (last game within 365 days). League offsets move with cross-league international games. Domestic log loss 0.6319 against 0.6343 for team Elo without players (−0.0024, −0.0045 to −0.0003).
  - Form (Predictive GLORY; sunset as a page 2026-10-02, still FORGE's input and a column on its register): each team's recency-weighted, opponent-adjusted stats before every game, carried over between seasons and shrunk toward average, scored by weights fit to predict the next game, and shown relative to the team's own league in Elo points. Every team in every league. Alone it does not beat Elo (domestic log loss +0.0015, −0.0010 to +0.0040; international +0.0451).
  - FORGE (renamed from GlorELO+ on 2026-10-02; `glorelo_plus.html` redirects to `forge.html`): Elo + Form within a league; Elo alone between leagues. Domestic backtest log loss 0.6291 (64.7% picked) against Elo's 0.6319; international games equal Elo's 0.6295. Weights in `prometheus/forge_weights.json` and `form_weights.json`, written by `--write-weights`; CI refits and warns when a blend weight moves more than 10%.
- **Site:** the header has two menus, **Teams** (GLORY, FORGE, Elo, then Sunset stats) and **Players** (Elo, AURA), each listing its stats by name with the question it answers. Home leads with FORGE (head-to-head box and top 10 teams playing now), then the top 10 of Team Elo and Player Elo; the margin lists every stat. One rankings page per metric, a Sunset stats page (including Record and Luck), one page per team (GLORY figure, year rank and season-end FORGE by season; current FORGE and Elo; last roster with player Elo and starters by season and role; Elo history), one page per listed player (Elo after every game, AURA by season with role rank, career by team stint, other names), a header search over teams and players (`teams.json`, `players.json`), a name filter on every rankings page (and a role filter on AURA), and 404, published daily to https://prometheus.thaiv.dev. Forecast pages open on current ratings for teams active in the last six months; the season picker switches to ratings at the end of each chosen year. The FORGE head-to-head uses Elo alone, with a note, for teams from different leagues.
- **AURA (player season stat, on the site since 2026-10-02):** `aura.html` lists 2,503 major-league player-seasons with 20+ games (a season needs about 22 games to reach 0.5 reliability), in win-chance points per game (25 × log-odds) with Role Z, role rank and a role filter; player pages show AURA by season. `prometheus/aura.py` scores each starter's share of a game: a logistic win-probability model on the five lanes' gaps to their lane opponent at 15 minutes (gold, XP, CS, kills, deaths, assists; one weight per role and stat, fit per year), and a player's AURA is their lane's 15-minute log-odds term plus a quarter of their team-centred change to 25 minutes (own change minus the teammates' average; 20 minutes if the game ended first). Snapshot models calibrated out of year (ECE 0.009 at 15, 0.007 at 25). In major-league player-seasons: split-half 0.59, r with teammates 0.24, next season on a new team 0.48 (lane gold gap 0.42, win % 0.27). Held out (weights from earlier years), the team-centred change beat the 15-minute term alone: new team +0.028 (+0.012 to +0.043), split-half +0.026. Summed over a team's lineups, players' AURA from one half predicts the other half's win % at 0.65, the same as the team's own AURA (0.66) but below GLORY (0.71, −0.058, −0.087 to −0.030) and win % (0.71): 15-minute lanes miss what decides the rest of the game. On one-starter substitutions, AURA so far adds nothing beyond Player Elo (r −0.006, −0.023 to +0.012). `scripts/evaluate_aura.py` → `docs/aura_report.md`; AURA's change rule is in `AGENTS.md`.
- **Predictions (2026-10-03):** `predictions.html` (linked as **Predictions** in the header) lists every scheduled pro match between two teams we rate, from Leaguepedia's schedule, with each team's chance of taking the series (and of one game) by FORGE within a major league, Elo across leagues and Elo within other leagues; past calls with their results, a "call" (right/missed), and a scorecard (series and games picked, log loss) split into calls saved before the match and calls reconstructed afterwards. Home shows the next 10 major-league and international matches under the FORGE head to head. Calls are kept in a log (`data/predictions.json`, backed up to S3 by CI), refreshed until a match starts and then frozen; `predictions.json` publishes it. On the first log (30-day backfill, 431 reconstructed series from 2 Sep 2026), the favourite won 65% of series and 61.5% of games, log loss 0.641 per game. `prometheus predict` prints the same odds in the terminal.
- **CLI:** `prometheus rankings glory|record|luck|forge|form|glorb` with league filters and years or year ranges (`--year 2021-2023`). Forecasts show now by default, or season-end ratings when years are given.

## Known limits

- Season stats cover only the four major leagues. Whether to extend them is an open product decision (`PRODUCT.md`). Form and Elo cover every league; FORGE shows major-league teams.
- Elo links regions only through international games. League offsets carry those results to every team in a region, but a league that rarely plays abroad still has a weakly measured offset. K=20, a between-season pull to the mean and an international K multiplier were tested and not adopted (see the work log).
- Form doesn't compare across regions (stats in a weaker league look better), so it is league-relative and FORGE falls back to Elo between leagues. FORGE therefore adds nothing over Elo for international games; the old GLORY+-based blend was 0.0007 better there (not significant).
- FORGE ratings mix regions in one table, but only same-league gaps translate directly into odds; cross-league odds come from Elo.
- Form's hyperparameters (half-life 20, carry 0.5, prior 5) were picked on the same backtest that reports them (a coarse 27-point grid; shrinkage barely mattered). Form's opponent adjustment and weights use only earlier or other seasons, but the blend curve's out-of-year fit is the only guard on the hyperparameters.
- Luck's earned win % comes from a linear per-game model; the season calibration removes its pull toward 50%, but Luck still repeats a little (0.34), so part of it is skill the stats miss.
- Record and Luck count games differently: Record counts every game that season (any league), Luck only major-league games.
- The 5-game minimum stays for every season stat, although the report says GLORY needs about 8 games and win % about 16 to reach 0.5 reliability.
- CI only warns when forecast weights drift; someone still has to run `--write-weights` and commit the files.
- A team page's current FORGE (fact line) uses today's Elo and Form, while its season row for this year uses the state after its last game; they can differ slightly.
- The CLI's `--sort-by` applies to GLORY and GLORB only.
- Player Elo moves all five starters together, so it records how a player's teams did and carries it through transfers; it does not split credit within a team. The player register and pages cover 4,854 players (ever in a major league or at an international event, or active in the last two years); past seasons in the register list only major-league player-seasons with 10+ games, to keep the page about 200KB gzipped.
- Each release is about 100MB (4,854 player pages, 2,087 team pages), and every page changes daily (footer date), so each deploy uploads the whole site and keeps a full copy under `releases/`.
- AURA credits a lane, not a player's whole game: a jungler's or support's score is their gap to the opposing jungler or support, kills and assists are shared across teammates, and with gold in the model the deaths weights come out positive (dying while staying even in gold is not penalized). Oracle's Elixir has no minute-by-minute data for LPL games in 2016–2017 and 2022–2025 and about half of 2021 (80% of 2026), so LPL players are missing from AURA in those years and Role Z and role ranks there compare LCK, LEC and LCS players only. Full-game stats (vision, damage, gold and kill shares) were tested on 2026-10-02 and mostly restate the team; within-team shares measure the role a team gives a player and don't travel (see the work log). The substitution test is confounded by how teams choose substitutes: the AURA difference doesn't predict even the raw result in swap games (r −0.011), though AURA so far predicts results in ordinary games (team gap r 0.29).
- About 1,850 team-games have no opponent row, so they get no Elo; Form counts them as unadjusted and GLORY's adjustment drops them (one team-season, so GLORY lists 603 against unadjusted GLORY's 604).
- The `before` date filter in `get_matches_frame` is exercised only by the backtest, not by its own test.
- **Predictions.** Leaguepedia rate-limits anonymous clients after a few quick calls and keeps refusing for several minutes; CI shares addresses with other users, so without the optional bot-password secrets a day's fetch can fail (the build then keeps yesterday's calls and results). Matches with a team Oracle's Elixir doesn't cover can't be rated (about half of all scheduled matches, nearly all in small leagues). Name matching depends on Leaguepedia and Oracle's Elixir names agreeing after folding; the three exceptions found so far are in `team_aliases.json`, and new ones need adding by hand (the build prints unmatched names). The calls use the data through the day before the build, and the build runs once a day, so a match may be called on ratings a day or two old and teams' rosters for that match are assumed to be their last ones. Reconstructed calls use earlier games only, but the forecast weights were fit on data that includes them. Series odds assume independent games and haven't been checked against past series yet. The whole log is rendered into `predictions.html` (about 650KB for 440 past matches), so it will need paging once it holds many months. League labels for events outside `EVENT_LEAGUES` come from the teams' home leagues, which can be a cross-league event (for example WSCI) the team played last.
- e2e tests need a built DB, so they only run locally and in the publish workflow after `setup_db.sh`.

## Future work

- **Series odds.** The Predictions page shows best-of-3/5 chances from the one-game chance, assuming independent games. Still to do: check that against past series (from the log, or from Oracle's Elixir games grouped into series), and add a best-of picker to the FORGE head-to-head box.
- **Predictions follow-ups.** Ask for a Leaguepedia bot password and set the CI secrets (higher rate limit). Page or collapse old results once the log holds a few months. A second scheduled run before the evening's matches (or a light job that only refreshes calls) so calls use the newest data. Report the unmatched names the build prints and grow `team_aliases.json`. Use the starting lineups when Leaguepedia has them, so a call knows about a substitute.
- **Form follow-ups.** Roster continuity from player data (carry more weight into a new season when the roster stays), and a finer hyperparameter search with a held-out period. (Early-game gold, XP, CS and kill diffs at 10/15/20 were tested on 2026-10-01 and gave no significant gain; see the work log.)
- **GLORY weights.** Team-season-level weights (one half's averages → the other half's win %, ridge, out of year) were tested on 2026-10-01 and gave no significant gain (see the work log). Ideas left: per-era standardization, fewer, more stable features, or a target with less luck than win % (for example the other half's Record).
- **Season-stat report centring.** Centre correlations within league-season as well as season, so stats that carry region strength (GLORY, Record) aren't penalised against win %, which averages 50% in every league.
- **Minimum games.** Use the season-stat report to set each stat's minimum (or show a reliability flag) instead of the shared 5-game cut.
- **Player Elo follow-ups.** Version 1 shipped 2026-10-02 (no synergy), with a player register and player pages. Next, each only under the significance rule: (a) familiarity, one fitted bonus that grows with games the five have played together; (b) role weights, team rating as a weighted mean by position; (c) pairwise synergy ratings, heavily shrunk. Also test a rookie start below league average, and pulling inactive players toward their league average. Still to do: individual credit within a team (role weights or AURA), which player Elo cannot give.
- **Release size.** An S3 lifecycle rule for old `releases/` (each about 100MB now), and only rewriting pages whose content changed (the footer date changes every page daily).
- **Elo follow-ups.** Tune the game-length curve (`lower_bound`, `center`). Measure each in the backtest, then refresh the forecast weights.
- **FORGE follow-ups.** Weight recent seasons more when fitting the blend. Extend FORGE to every region (Form already covers them; between leagues it is Elo already); ship only if a backtest on non-major domestic games shows it beats Elo there by more than noise. If it does, Team Elo could become a column on the FORGE page.
- **Market benchmark (Kalshi, Polymarket).** The market is the strongest public forecast, so it is the real test of whether FORGE works. Prediction markets publish prices through public APIs (Polymarket's Gamma and CLOB APIs; Kalshi's trade API), and a contract's price is already a probability with no bookmaker margin to strip, only the bid-ask spread. Plan: (1) check which LoL markets each lists (series winners by league; likely LCK, LPL, LEC, LCS and international events, little below that) and their terms of use; (2) at each daily build, record each listed market's mid price for the matches in the prediction log (`prometheus/schedule.py`), keyed by Leaguepedia match id, with the time taken; record the last price before the start as the closing line; (3) score the market and FORGE on the same series (accuracy, Brier, log loss on series results, paired bootstrap), and show the market's chance beside ours on the Predictions page. Thin markets (wide spread, little volume) should be flagged or left out. Live (in-game) prices don't compare with a pre-match forecast; use only prices from before the start. The daily build snapshots once a day, so a closing line needs a snapshot nearer the start (a second scheduled run, or a small job that only polls prices). Historical prices for a backtest over past seasons would need each API's price history; check how far back it goes.
- **Backtest report.** Add month-cluster bootstrap intervals for accuracy and paired accuracy differences to `evaluate_metrics.py`, so the report shows every interval quoted on the site.
- **Tests.** A fixture-DB test for the `before` filter; run e2e tests against a small fixture DB so they don't need the full download.
- **Cleanup.** Remove the `if __name__ == "__main__":` scratch blocks. `notebooks/win_prediction.ipynb` imports the removed `win_prediction.py`; update it to `aura.py` or delete it, and drop `xgboost` from the dependencies if nothing else needs it.
- **AURA.** Published 2026-10-02 (snapshot model, team-centred late change, season page and player-page register). Next: (a) a season-level check in `evaluate_aura.py` that the published season values (not just per-game scores) stay reliable as data grows, and a note on the AURA page when the current year is short of 20 games for most players; (b) done 2026-10-02: the team-centred 15→25 change at a quarter weight (snapshot means, share-split swings, full-game gaps and role-specific stat sets were tested and not adopted); (c) credit after 15 minutes is still missing (roster AURA 0.65 vs GLORY 0.71): Oracle's Elixir has no player-level objectives, so this needs a different source or a better team-level split; vision and frontline gaps (vspm, wcpm, damage taken and mitigated) showed some player signal on new teams against their team-only control (+0.065, −0.007 to +0.132), worth a retest as data grows; (d) a Predictive AURA (recency-weighted player states, as Form does for teams) and folding it into Player Elo, either as a credit split of each game's Elo change (team forecasts then move only on roster changes, so it needs a backtest on games after roster changes; the substitution test is too confounded to stand in for it) or as a FORGE-style blend; ship only under the significance rule.
- **Champions and matchups in AURA.** `player_stats.champion` is stored and `load_aura_games` already reads it. Use it so a player is judged against the matchup they drew: the expected lane gap for their champion against the lane opponent's champion (per year, shrunk toward each champion's average gap when a pairing is rare), with AURA taken on the gap above that expectation. Then report champion pools (games, AURA by champion) on player pages, and test draft-based features (team compositions, lane matchups) in the forecasts.
