# Current state

Last reviewed: 2026-10-03 (Predictions page). This is a code baseline. Read `db/prometheus.db` or the live site for current rankings.

## Working

- **Data:** Oracle's Elixir CSVs for 2014 to the present, rebuilt from scratch by `scripts/setup_db.sh` and refreshed daily in CI. International events (Worlds, MSI, EWC, First Stand, and since 2026-10-06 the Demacia Cup Global Invitational `DCGI` and World Star Challengers Invitational `WSCI`, ...) are ingested as their own leagues (`INTERNATIONAL_LEAGUES`); they are the only games that connect regions, and never a team's home league.
- **Two families.** Season stats describe a team-season with hindsight and are judged by `scripts/evaluate_season_stats.py` (stability and fit to that season's results; `docs/season_stats_report.md`). Forecasts predict the next game from earlier games only and are judged by `scripts/evaluate_metrics.py` (`docs/metric_backtest.md`). No forecast uses a season stat as input.
- **Season stats** (LCK/LPL/LEC/LCS team-seasons):
  - GLORY: per-year regression weights on gold and objective rates, with every game's stats adjusted for the opponent's full-season Record; about 0–100, with Era Z and League Z. Full-season reliability 0.91; about 8 games to reach 0.5.
  - Record (sunset 2026-10-01; page kept, still GLORY's opponent-strength input): a schedule-adjusted results rating (Bradley-Terry on game-length-weighted results, every league including international events), shown as the chance to beat the average major-league team that year. Reliability 0.89.
  - Luck (sunset 2026-10-01; page kept): wins above what a team's play earned (GLORY's per-game model, calibrated per season). Reliability 0.34, so it is mostly luck; it correlates 0.23 with win %.
  - Sunset (`sunset.html`, linked from the header's Teams menu): Record, Luck, GLORB, GLORY unadjusted (`glory_unadjusted.html`), Form (folded into FORGE), and GLORY+ (folded into GLORY; `glory_plus.html` redirects).
- **Forecasts:**
  - Game-length Elo for every team in every region, built from player ratings: a team's rating is the average of its five starters plus its league offset; new players start at the average of their league's active players (last game within 365 days). League offsets move with cross-league international games. A team's home league is its most-played league this year, and forecasts rate it by its main roster (its last home-league or international lineup). Domestic log loss 0.6320 against 0.6347 for team Elo without players (−0.0027, −0.0047 to −0.0006).
  - Form (Predictive GLORY; sunset as a page 2026-10-02, still FORGE's input and a column on its register): each team's recency-weighted, opponent-adjusted stats before every game, carried over between seasons and shrunk toward average, scored by weights fit to predict the next game, and shown relative to the team's own league in Elo points. Every team in every league. Alone it does not beat Elo (domestic log loss +0.0014, −0.0010 to +0.0038; international +0.0450).
  - FORGE (renamed from GlorELO+ on 2026-10-02; `glorelo_plus.html` redirects to `forge.html`): Elo + Form within a league; Elo alone between leagues. Domestic backtest log loss 0.6292 (64.8% picked) against Elo's 0.6320; international games equal Elo's 0.6299. Weights in `prometheus/forge_weights.json` and `form_weights.json`, written by `--write-weights`; CI refits and warns when a blend weight moves more than 10%.
- **Site:** the header has two menus, **Teams** (GLORY, FORGE, Elo, then Sunset stats) and **Players** (Elo, AURA), each listing its stats by name with the question it answers. Home leads with FORGE (head-to-head box and top 10 teams playing now), then the top 10 of Team Elo and Player Elo; the margin lists every stat. One rankings page per metric, a Sunset stats page (including Record and Luck), one page per team (Elo history; GLORY figure, year rank and season-end FORGE by season; last roster with player Elo and starters by season and role; every series and game with our pre-game call, Kalshi's price, Elo change and the starting five), one page per listed player (Elo after every game, AURA by season with role rank, career by team stint, other names, every series and game with team, champion, per-game AURA, our call, Kalshi and the player's Elo change), each with an "On this page" contents list, a header search over teams and players (`teams.json`, `players.json`), a name filter on every rankings page (and a role filter on AURA), and 404, published daily to https://prometheus.thaiv.dev. Forecast pages open on current ratings for teams active in the last six months; the season picker switches to ratings at the end of each chosen year. The FORGE head-to-head uses Elo alone, with a note, for teams from different leagues.
- **AURA (player season stat, on the site since 2026-10-02):** `aura.html` lists 2,503 major-league player-seasons with 20+ games (a season needs about 22 games to reach 0.5 reliability), in win-chance points per game (25 × log-odds) with Role Z, role rank and a role filter; player pages show AURA by season. `prometheus/aura.py` scores each starter's share of a game: a logistic win-probability model on the five lanes' gaps to their lane opponent at 15 minutes (gold, XP, CS, kills, deaths, assists; one weight per role and stat, fit per year), and a player's AURA is their lane's 15-minute log-odds term plus a quarter of their team-centred change to 25 minutes (own change minus the teammates' average; 20 minutes if the game ended first). Snapshot models calibrated out of year (ECE 0.009 at 15, 0.007 at 25). In major-league player-seasons: split-half 0.59, r with teammates 0.24, next season on a new team 0.48 (lane gold gap 0.42, win % 0.27). Held out (weights from earlier years), the team-centred change beat the 15-minute term alone: new team +0.028 (+0.012 to +0.043), split-half +0.026. Summed over a team's lineups, players' AURA from one half predicts the other half's win % at 0.65, the same as the team's own AURA (0.66) but below GLORY (0.71, −0.058, −0.087 to −0.030) and win % (0.71): 15-minute lanes miss what decides the rest of the game. On one-starter substitutions, AURA so far adds nothing beyond Player Elo (r −0.006, −0.023 to +0.012). `scripts/evaluate_aura.py` → `docs/aura_report.md`; AURA's change rule is in `AGENTS.md`.
- **Predictions (2026-10-03):** `predictions.html` (linked as **Predictions** in the header; the major leagues and international events by default, every league on request) lists every scheduled pro match between two teams we rate, from Leaguepedia's schedule, with each team's chance of taking the series (and of one game) by FORGE within a major league, Elo across leagues and Elo within other leagues; past calls with their results, a "call" (right/missed), a scorecard (series and games picked, log loss) split into calls saved before the match and calls reconstructed afterwards, and *against the market* (2026-10-06): our series calls against Kalshi's last pre-start price on settled priced matches, FORGE and Elo apart, with the log-loss difference and its interval from 30 series a row, plus paper bets by edge, both split into saved calls (the forward record) and the backtest of rebuilt calls ($1 on our side 12 hours out where our chance beat the ask by more than 0/3/5/10 points: return after fees and CLV, FORGE and Elo apart) and the alerts' record (closing line value, share beating the close, ROI after fees). Home shows the next 10 major-league and international matches under the FORGE head to head. Each upcoming match that Kalshi lists also shows the market's series chance (a Kalshi column and a caret on the duel bar), refreshed about hourly between builds through `kalshi.json` and frozen at the start like our call (2026-10-06). Calls are kept in a log (`data/predictions.json`, backed up to S3 by CI), refreshed until a match starts and then frozen; `predictions.json` publishes it. The log runs from 1 Jan 2026 (rebuilt calls before 4 Oct, extended by `scripts/extend_log.py` on 2026-10-06): the favourite won 67.3% of 4,204 rebuilt series, log loss 0.638 per game. The page lists the last 3 days' results; older ones are on `results/YYYY-MM.html`. `prometheus predict` prints the same odds in the terminal.
- **Market benchmark (2026-10-04):** `scripts/evaluate_markets.py` → `docs/market_report.md` scores our reconstructed match calls against Kalshi's pre-match prices on 2,156 series (Dec 2025 to Sep 2026; Kalshi lists nearly every pro series, majors to ERLs and academies). In major leagues and international events, 12 hours before the start, FORGE ties the market (log loss 0.5816 vs 0.5822, Δ −0.0006, −0.016 to +0.015, 708 series); at the close the market is ahead (+0.013, −0.004 to +0.029) because it moves on late news (+0.020 log loss in the last 12 hours). Elsewhere the market is clearly better: Elo within other leagues +0.049 at 12 hours, Elo across leagues +0.164. A fit of results on both forecasts puts our weight near zero (0.01, −0.18 to +0.17): the market already prices what we know. Map 1 against our one-game chance: FORGE +0.004 (−0.007 to +0.015), other leagues +0.037. Our series chances are as well calibrated as the market's in major leagues (ECE 0.030 vs 0.043).
- **Kalshi alerts (2026-10-06; hourly since 2026-10-06):** the daily build and an hourly price job post a `kalshi-alert` GitHub issue (with an @-mention, so GitHub emails it) when FORGE's series chance beats Kalshi's ask by more than 5 points on a match 6–36 hours out: a table of the day's matches at current prices (an edge that later falls under 5 points is marked gone), a comment, which GitHub emails, for each match new since the last run, links to Kalshi, the Predictions page and both teams, and a running paper record of earlier alerts as $1 bets after the 7% taker fee, with closing line value. Every alert is recorded in the prediction log, so the record covers calls genuinely made before the match. Basis: in the backtest, FORGE's value bets above 5 points returned +12.9% after taker fees (−5.0% to +31.2%, 215 bets), and FORGE's weight beside the market's price 6–24 hours out was about 0.55 (interval above 0); neither is proof.
- **CLI:** `prometheus rankings glory|record|luck|forge|form|glorb` with league filters and years or year ranges (`--year 2021-2023`). Forecasts show now by default, or season-end ratings when years are given.

## Known limits

- Season stats cover only the four major leagues. Whether to extend them is an open product decision (`PRODUCT.md`). Form and Elo cover every league; FORGE shows major-league teams.
- Elo links regions only through international games. League offsets carry those results to every team in a region, but a league that rarely plays abroad still has a weakly measured offset. K=20, a between-season pull to the mean and an international K multiplier were tested and not adopted (see the work log).
- Form doesn't compare across regions (stats in a weaker league look better), so it is league-relative and FORGE falls back to Elo between leagues. FORGE therefore adds nothing over Elo for international games; the old GLORY+-based blend was 0.0007 better there (not significant).
- FORGE ratings mix regions in one table, but only same-league gaps translate directly into odds; cross-league odds come from Elo.
- Form's hyperparameters (half-life 20, carry 0.5, prior 5) were picked on the same backtest that reports them (a coarse 27-point grid; shrinkage barely mattered). Form's opponent adjustment and weights use only earlier or other seasons, but the blend curve's out-of-year fit is the only guard on the hyperparameters.
- Luck's earned win % comes from a linear per-game model; the season calibration removes its pull toward 50%, but Luck still repeats a little (0.34), so part of it is skill the stats miss.
- Record and Luck count games differently: Record counts every game that season (any league), Luck only major-league games.
- The 5-game minimum stays for every season stat, although the report says GLORY needs about 8 games and win % about 16 to reach 0.5 reliability.
- CI only warns when forecast weights drift; someone still has to run `--write-weights` and commit the files.
- A team page's current FORGE (fact line) uses today's Elo and Form, while its season row for this year uses the state after its last game; they can differ slightly.
- The CLI's `--sort-by` applies to GLORY and GLORB only.
- Player Elo moves all five starters together, so it records how a player's teams did and carries it through transfers; it does not split credit within a team. The player register and pages cover 4,854 players (ever in a major league or at an international event, or active in the last two years); past seasons in the register list only major-league player-seasons with 10+ games, to keep the page about 200KB gzipped.
- Each release is about 290MB (4,854 player pages, 2,087 team pages, and 117MB of game-log JSON since 2026-10-06), and every page changes daily (footer date), so each deploy uploads the whole site and keeps a full copy under `releases/`.
- **Game logs.** Series are inferred (a team's consecutive games against one opponent within 12 hours), and so is the best-of behind the series chance (a 2–0 Bo2 is called as a Bo3; a 1–1 gets no series chance). Pre-game calls use today's published weights for every past game. Kalshi prices on past series come from `data/market_prices.json`, refreshed only by rerunning `evaluate_markets.py` and `export_market_prices.py` and uploading the file; without it in CI, logs show only prices saved in the prediction log.
- AURA credits a lane, not a player's whole game: a jungler's or support's score is their gap to the opposing jungler or support, kills and assists are shared across teammates, and with gold in the model the deaths weights come out positive (dying while staying even in gold is not penalized). Oracle's Elixir has no minute-by-minute data for LPL games in 2016–2017 and 2022–2025 and about half of 2021 (80% of 2026), so LPL players are missing from AURA in those years and Role Z and role ranks there compare LCK, LEC and LCS players only. Full-game stats (vision, damage, gold and kill shares) were tested on 2026-10-02 and mostly restate the team; within-team shares measure the role a team gives a player and don't travel (see the work log). The substitution test is confounded by how teams choose substitutes: the AURA difference doesn't predict even the raw result in swap games (r −0.011), though AURA so far predicts results in ordinary games (team gap r 0.29).
- Oracle's Elixir files the first games of a new season (year 2027 since 6 Oct 2026) in the current year's file. GLORY skips a year until a team has 5 games in it; Elo, Form and the registers take those games as they come.
- About 1,850 team-games have no opponent row, so they get no Elo; Form counts them as unadjusted and GLORY's adjustment drops them (one team-season, so GLORY lists 603 against unadjusted GLORY's 604).
- The `before` date filter in `get_matches_frame` is exercised only by the backtest, not by its own test.
- **Predictions.** Leaguepedia rate-limits anonymous clients after a few quick calls and keeps refusing for several minutes; CI shares addresses with other users; the bot-password secrets are set (2026-10-06) for a higher limit, but a day's fetch can still fail (the build then keeps yesterday's calls and results). Matches with a team Oracle's Elixir doesn't cover can't be rated (about half of all scheduled matches, nearly all in small leagues). Name matching depends on Leaguepedia and Oracle's Elixir names agreeing after folding; the three exceptions found so far are in `team_aliases.json`, and new ones need adding by hand (the build prints unmatched names). The calls use the data through the day before the build, and the build runs once a day, so a match may be called on ratings a day or two old and teams' rosters for that match are assumed to be their last ones. Reconstructed calls use earlier games only, but the forecast weights were fit on data that includes them. Series odds assume independent games and haven't been checked against past series yet. The whole log is rendered into `predictions.html` (about 650KB for 440 past matches), so it will need paging once it holds many months. League labels for events outside `EVENT_LEAGUES` come from the teams' home leagues; a new cross-region event missing from `INTERNATIONAL_LEAGUES` becomes the home league of the teams that play it (as DCGI and WSCI did until 2026-10-06), which turns their FORGE calls into Elo calls and drops them from FORGE and alerts.
- **Kalshi alerts** run about hourly (GitHub may start scheduled runs late, and an hour is skipped while the site is publishing); prices can move before you see the email. The calls they compare against still come from the daily build. The issues are public (the repo is). The 5-point bar and the 6–36 hour window were set from one backtest that tried several strategies.
- **Kalshi prices on the page** come from `kalshi.json`, refreshed about hourly (Kalshi's API refuses in-browser requests); without JS a visitor sees the daily build's prices; matches logged before 2026-10-06 have none. Whether Kalshi's Developer Agreement allows republishing prices hasn't been checked (its page refused the fetch).
- **Market benchmark.** Our calls in it are reconstructed (ratings from the day before) with forecast weights fit on data that includes those matches, which flatters us a little. Kalshi's start times for some markets give only the day (taken as midnight Eastern, so the "close" price is earlier than it could be). 52 Kalshi team names are still unmatched after the aliases, nearly all teams Oracle's Elixir lacks (national teams, small ERLs). Six series where Kalshi's result disagreed with our games were dropped (cause not checked). Candlestick fetching is slow on a cold cache (Kalshi refuses bursts).
- e2e tests need a built DB, so they only run locally and in the publish workflow after `setup_db.sh`.

## Future work

- **Game log follow-ups.** Refresh Kalshi prices automatically (export them from the daily build's own fetches, or rerun the benchmark on a schedule) instead of a hand-uploaded file; take the best-of from Leaguepedia where the prediction log has the match; show our saved call (from the prediction log) beside the reconstructed one; a champion pool summary on player pages; and, if the release size matters, gzip the game-log JSON at upload with `Content-Encoding`, or expire old releases (see deployment Notes).

- **Series odds.** The Predictions page shows best-of-3/5 chances from the one-game chance, assuming independent games. Still to do: check that against past series (from the log, or from Oracle's Elixir games grouped into series), and add a best-of picker to the FORGE head-to-head box.
- **Predictions follow-ups.** Page or collapse old results once the log holds a few months. A second scheduled run before the evening's matches (or a light job that only refreshes calls) so calls use the newest data. Report the unmatched names the build prints and grow `team_aliases.json`. Use the starting lineups when Leaguepedia has them, so a call knows about a substitute.
- **Form follow-ups.** Roster continuity from player data (carry more weight into a new season when the roster stays), and a finer hyperparameter search with a held-out period. (Early-game gold, XP, CS and kill diffs at 10/15/20 were tested on 2026-10-01 and gave no significant gain; see the work log.)
- **GLORY weights.** Team-season-level weights (one half's averages → the other half's win %, ridge, out of year) were tested on 2026-10-01 and gave no significant gain (see the work log). Ideas left: per-era standardization, fewer, more stable features, or a target with less luck than win % (for example the other half's Record).
- **Season-stat report centring.** Centre correlations within league-season as well as season, so stats that carry region strength (GLORY, Record) aren't penalised against win %, which averages 50% in every league.
- **Minimum games.** Use the season-stat report to set each stat's minimum (or show a reliability flag) instead of the shared 5-game cut.
- **Player Elo follow-ups.** Version 1 shipped 2026-10-02 (no synergy), with a player register and player pages. Next, each only under the significance rule: (a) familiarity, one fitted bonus that grows with games the five have played together; (b) role weights, team rating as a weighted mean by position; (c) pairwise synergy ratings, heavily shrunk. Also test a rookie start below league average, and pulling inactive players toward their league average. Still to do: individual credit within a team (role weights or AURA), which player Elo cannot give.
- **Release size.** An S3 lifecycle rule for old `releases/` (each about 100MB now), and only rewriting pages whose content changed (the footer date changes every page daily).
- **Elo follow-ups.** Margins of victory tested 2026-10-06 (`scripts/elo_variants.py`, `docs/elo_variants_*.md`): gold, kills, objectives, learned composites, a re-tuned game-length curve and K; none beat the published game-length margin on held-out seasons. Closest: gold share and the learned dominance index (about −0.0005 to −0.0009 domestic, intervals spanning 0). The re-tuned curve (lower 0.55, center 25 min) helped international games (−0.0025, −0.0044 to −0.0005) but not domestic ones; if anything is revisited, test that alone with a fresh held-out season (2027).
- **FORGE follow-ups.** Weight recent seasons more when fitting the blend. Extend FORGE to every region (Form already covers them; between leagues it is Elo already); ship only if a backtest on non-major domestic games shows it beats Elo there by more than noise. If it does, Team Elo could become a column on the FORGE page.
- **Cross-league odds outside the majors.** The benchmark is in (`evaluate_metrics.py`, "Cross-league, every league"), and the home-league and main-roster fixes shipped (work log 2026-10-06). Linking leagues at EMEA Masters, cups and promotion cuts cross-league log loss by a further 0.014–0.017 but makes major-league forecasts significantly worse since 2022, because promoted players carry minor-league offsets into the majors. Next: convert a player's rating on a move between leagues by their standing in the old league rather than by offsets, then retest the links. The market still beats us across leagues (+0.085 log loss 12 hours out).
- **Kalshi alert follow-ups.** After two or three months of alerts, compare the paper record with the backtest; Revisit the 5-point bar only on the forward record, not the backtest.
- **Market benchmark follow-ups.** The backtest is in (`scripts/evaluate_markets.py`, see Working). Next: (a) record Kalshi's mid price at the time each call is saved (and the last before the start) in the prediction log, so saved calls are compared at the same moment rather than reconstructed ones; (b) small leagues and cross-league calls are where the market beats us most (Elo within other leagues +0.049 log loss 12 hours out, cross-league Elo +0.164): test a pull toward the league mean for inactive teams, roster continuity, and a better cross-league curve there, each under the significance rule; (c) most of the market's gain in the last 12 hours is late news (lineups); starting lineups from Leaguepedia (see Predictions follow-ups) are the matching input for us; (d) done 2026-10-06: the Predictions page shows Kalshi's chance (read Kalshi's Developer Agreement on redistributing prices before relying on it; `KALSHI_PRICES=0` turns it off); the comparison table is on the page (2026-10-06), with Kalshi's last pre-start price backfilled from its price history for the matches logged before hourly reads began (`scripts/backfill_market_prices.py`, 2026-10-06); next, backfilling closing prices for past logged matches from the candlesticks cache; (e) add `INTZ e-Sports` and other new names to `MARKET_ALIASES` as they show up; (f) Polymarket also lists per-match LoL markets, down to ERLs, but many with no order book; not yet read.
- **Backtest report.** Add month-cluster bootstrap intervals for accuracy and paired accuracy differences to `evaluate_metrics.py`, so the report shows every interval quoted on the site.
- **Tests.** A fixture-DB test for the `before` filter; run e2e tests against a small fixture DB so they don't need the full download.
- **Cleanup.** Remove the `if __name__ == "__main__":` scratch blocks. `notebooks/win_prediction.ipynb` imports the removed `win_prediction.py`; update it to `aura.py` or delete it, and drop `xgboost` from the dependencies if nothing else needs it.
- **AURA.** Published 2026-10-02 (snapshot model, team-centred late change, season page and player-page register). Next: (a) a season-level check in `evaluate_aura.py` that the published season values (not just per-game scores) stay reliable as data grows, and a note on the AURA page when the current year is short of 20 games for most players; (b) done 2026-10-02: the team-centred 15→25 change at a quarter weight (snapshot means, share-split swings, full-game gaps and role-specific stat sets were tested and not adopted); (c) credit after 15 minutes is still missing (roster AURA 0.65 vs GLORY 0.71): Oracle's Elixir has no player-level objectives, so this needs a different source or a better team-level split; vision and frontline gaps (vspm, wcpm, damage taken and mitigated) showed some player signal on new teams against their team-only control (+0.065, −0.007 to +0.132), worth a retest as data grows; (d) a Predictive AURA (recency-weighted player states, as Form does for teams) and folding it into Player Elo, either as a credit split of each game's Elo change (team forecasts then move only on roster changes, so it needs a backtest on games after roster changes; the substitution test is too confounded to stand in for it) or as a FORGE-style blend; ship only under the significance rule.
- **Champions and matchups in AURA.** `player_stats.champion` is stored and `load_aura_games` already reads it. Use it so a player is judged against the matchup they drew: the expected lane gap for their champion against the lane opponent's champion (per year, shrunk toward each champion's average gap when a pairing is rare), with AURA taken on the gap above that expectation. Then report champion pools (games, AURA by champion) on player pages, and test draft-based features (team compositions, lane matchups) in the forecasts.
