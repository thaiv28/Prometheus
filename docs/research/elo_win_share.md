## Fixed protocol

elo_win_share.py: individual in-game credit in player-built Elo.

Fixed before held-out scoring: gold, XP, CS and equal gold+XP z-score at 15/20m,
plus AURA (earlier_only=True); lambda 0/.025/.05/.10. Role scaling uses strictly
prior calendar years (at least 100 observations per role); no current-year fit.
Tune games-weighted domestic + within-other Elo loss through 2021, as
elo_new_players.py. Select once, then report 2022+ with refitted out-of-year
Elo/FORGE curves and Form weights using the existing harness. No DB writes.

w = max(0, .2 + lambda * result_sign * (z - team_mean)), normalized.
Both complete five-player sides are required; otherwise the whole game falls
back to equal shares. At lambda=0 replay is exactly published. The engine
renormalizes after published rookie/away multipliers to preserve their total
move; with identical multipliers m this is (m * raw_change) * 5 * w.

Training uses seasons ≤2021; held out 2022–2026 (partial final year). This is retrospective: prior experiments have viewed these years. Harness curves use other years within the requested split, not strictly earlier years; AURA and contribution scaling alone use strictly earlier years. Paired intervals resample games unless stated otherwise; they do not correct for correlated series or prior research.

## Interpretation and limits

The fixed-roster invariance claim needs qualification: published provisional and away multipliers differ across starters. Renormalizing the weighted steps preserves each update’s baseline total at the same pre-game state. Across a full replay, transfers, league-average rookie starts and international offsets propagate differences even into games without an immediate roster change. Role-specific earlier-year scales prevent scale differences alone from allocating all credit to one role; season-end role drift is reported rather than assumed absent.

Shares use match result to orient credit, not the sign of Elo change: a heavy favorite can lose Elo in a long win. Clipping prevents reversing a player’s underlying move. AURA uses its earlier-only snapshot fits, with the existing late-snapshot fallback; no same-year AURA weights enter the updates. Contribution data from the current match affects only subsequent predictions.
